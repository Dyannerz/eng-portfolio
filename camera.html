Project Objective

Engineering an accessible alternative to expensive broadcast robotics

This project focused on developing an affordable robotic camera system for cage-side MMA filming capable of achieving smooth cinematic movement while overcoming common broadcast obstructions such as referees, cage fencing, and fighter movement. Existing robotic broadcast systems were extremely expensive, bulky, and impractical for smaller venues, so the goal was to engineer a compact and portable alternative optimized for combat sports environments.

Engineering Design Process

From requirements to concept selection

The project began with extensive research into professional robotic filming systems including cable cameras, rail cameras, and multi-axis robotic arms. Using Pugh charts, and morphological charts, multiple candidate concepts were evaluated based on:

  • Mechanical reach
  • Portability
  • Cost
  • Stability
  • Motion smoothness
  • Ease of operation

The final design evolved into a semi-autonomous mobile robotic arm system featuring:

  • Tank drive mobility
  • Telescopic extension arm
  • Multi axis wrist control
  • Custom planetary gearboxes
  • Wireless control architecture
  • Line following autonomous navigation

Mechanical System Design

Four integrated subsystems working in harmony

The robot was designed as four integrated subsystems:

  • Drivetrain
  • Rotating arm joint
  • Telescopic arm
  • 3-axis camera wrist

The drivetrain used a compact differential tank drive system with independent motor control for precise movement around the octagon perimeter. The original rail based concept was later redesigned into an autonomous line following platform using color sensors and onboard control logic to improve portability and reduce setup complexity.

The telescopic arm used nested aluminum tubing, constant force springs, cable driven actuation, and custom gearbox reductions to achieve smooth extension while maintaining structural stiffness and low weight.

Performance & Cost Analysis

Professional capability at accessible price point

Commercial robotic broadcast systems such as Spidercam and Mo-Sys typically range from approximately $80,000–$350,000 USD, while the complete Fight Snapper system was designed with a total manufacturing cost of approximately $6,000 USD. This created a significantly more accessible solution for smaller productions and independent videographers while maintaining many of the core cinematic movement capabilities of high-end systems.

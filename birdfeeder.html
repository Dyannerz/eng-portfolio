Project Objective

Engineering an affordable solo training solution for badminton athletes

This project focused on developing a portable automated badminton shuttlecock launcher capable of simulating realistic badminton shots for solo training. The goal was to engineer an affordable and customizable alternative to commercial badminton machines by integrating mechanical systems, embedded electronics, and wireless control into a compact training platform.

The launcher was designed to generate multiple shot types including clears, drives, and smashes while allowing wireless control through a smartphone interface. The project combined mechanical design, motor systems, embedded programming, and rapid prototyping into a complete mechatronics system.

Core challenge: Deliver consistent shuttlecock launching with adjustable speed and trajectory at a fraction of commercial machine costs.

Engineering Design Process

From concept evaluation to functional prototype

The project began with research into commercial badminton launchers and flywheel-based ball launching systems. Multiple concepts were evaluated based on:

  • Shot consistency
  • Launch speed
  • Portability
  • Mechanical simplicity
  • Cost
  • Wireless control capability

Several launch mechanisms were explored before selecting a dual-wheel flywheel system powered by high-speed brushless motors. This design provided reliable shuttle acceleration, adjustable launch speed, and compact packaging.

The development process involved:

  • CAD prototyping
  • Mechanical testing
  • ESC and motor integration
  • Embedded software development
  • Launch consistency testing
  • Iterative redesign and optimization

Mechanical System Design

Dual flywheel launch mechanism for shot versatility

The launcher used two counter-rotating high-speed wheels to accelerate the shuttlecock through frictional contact. Wheel spacing and alignment were iteratively refined to improve:

  • Shuttle stability
  • Launch consistency
  • Shot accuracy
  • Feather durability

An adjustable aiming mechanism allowed the launcher to vary shot trajectory and simulate different badminton shots such as:

  • Clears
  • Drives
  • Net shots
  • Smash trajectories

The frame structure was designed to remain lightweight while maintaining rigidity under high motor speeds and vibration loads.

Electrical & Embedded Systems

ESP32-based control with Bluetooth smartphone interface

The electrical system integrated:

  • Brushless DC motors
  • Electronic Speed Controllers (ESCs)
  • ESP32 microcontroller
  • Bluetooth wireless communication
  • Portable battery power

The ESP32 acted as the central controller, handling:

  • Motor speed control
  • Bluetooth communication
  • User input processing
  • Launch timing logic

Custom embedded software was developed using the Arduino framework to provide stable motor control and wireless operation through a smartphone interface.

Wireless control: The smartphone app connects via Bluetooth to the ESP32, allowing users to adjust motor speeds, control servo angles for aiming, and trigger launches remotely — enabling solo training without a partner.

Testing & Iteration

Refining shot consistency and system reliability

Extensive testing was conducted to improve:

  • Shot repeatability
  • Motor synchronization
  • Shuttle stability
  • Structural vibration
  • Launch angle consistency

Several engineering challenges were encountered including shuttle wobble, uneven wheel traction, and high-speed vibration. These were solved through iterative redesigns, ESC tuning, and structural improvements.

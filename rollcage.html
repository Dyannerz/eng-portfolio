Design Process

Topology‑driven development from solid block to tubular frame

The design methodology began by defining a solid aluminum block envelope covering the minimum driver head clearance up to the canopy inner surface. Key attachment points to the main chassis and primary load paths were identified, then the entire geometry was imported into ANSYS Mechanical.

All relevant load cases (frontal impact, rollover, side intrusion) were applied, and a topology optimization study was performed. The resulting organic shape suggested an efficient material distribution — acting as a blueprint for the tubular spaceframe layout.

📐 Topology optimization insight: the solver naturally removed material from low‑stress zones, revealing a truss‑like configuration that inspired the final tube routing and joint placements.

After extracting the optimal shape, the roll cage was translated into a tubular CAD model using CATIA. Multiple iterations were then executed to fine‑tune geometry, joint angles, and tube diameters while respecting manufacturing constraints.

Iterative Refinement

From initial prototype to final high‑margin design

Iteration 1 — side bar clearance: The initial rough geometry highlighted an ergonomic issue: the side bars restricted driver movement. To improve ingress/egress and comfort, the side bars were bent outward slightly, creating more shoulder room while maintaining structural stiffness.

Safety factor remained above 1.0 in most zones, but the main hoop exhibited localized stress concentrations near the bend radius.

Iteration 2 — hoop optimization: The main hoop was showing a safety factor below 1.0. The solution involved increasing the bend radius, which reduced stress concentration dramatically. Additionally, a diagonal support bar linking the hoop to the side structure was introduced, improving load distribution.

These changes raised the minimum safety factor to >1.1 in the hoop region, but shear stress at the top attachment required further attention.

Iteration 4–7 — vertical strut angling: A vertical bar supporting the top hoop was added, but early FEA revealed excessive shear stress at the top joint. By re‑angling the strut to approximately 45° relative to the backstay and main hoop, the load transferred more efficiently into the main roll structure. A cross bar was added at the top plane and the rear diagonal was removed (which previously caused undesirable stress peaks).

✅ Design evolution: After 17 distinct iterations — each addressing stress risers, driver packaging, and manufacturing feasibility — every load case achieved a safety factor above the regulation threshold of 1.1. The final assembly weighs only 10.7 kg, a 35% reduction from the previous GEN11 design (16.48 kg).

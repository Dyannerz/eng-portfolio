Project Objective

Core material reduction without compromising structural soundness

The goal was to remove core material and the subsequent two ply layers to achieve substantial weight savings. Since the entire car is manufactured from composites, experimenting with core placement and behaviour was a high-value initiative. No race regulations apply to the aerobody's structural integrity, and there are no direct forces acting on the surface — providing complete design freedom. The only constraints were to prevent excessive deformation under acceleration and dynamic loads, and to avoid introducing additional stiffeners.

🎯 Design freedom: No structural regulations + no direct surface forces → full flexibility to optimize core layout for minimum weight while ensuring adequate stiffness under 1g acceleration, gravity, and dynamic cornering loads.

Design Process & Baseline Analysis

Baseline assessment: The original BAB showed up to 9 mm deformation near the rear (butt) section, but was significantly over-engineered in other regions. Core material could therefore be removed from low-stress areas without affecting performance.

Core material selection: Thick Rohacell 200 was used for high-load areas (chassis bottom), while ¼ or ⅛ Divinycell was applied to the remaining body. The choice of core type and thickness was determined by local stress distribution from FEA.

Manufacturability check: Each ply region was validated using CATIA Composites Design producibility tools (see 3.png).

ANSYS Simulation Setup

  • All components contacting the BAB were included (top aerobody excluded as it attaches to chassis).
  • Bonded contacts: stiffeners and chassis to BAB.
  • Fixed supports at suspension clevises (hardpoints).
  • Point masses: driver, battery box, roll cage.
  • Outward pressure applied to simulate Bernoulli’s principle (aerodynamic suction).
  • 1g forward acceleration + gravity.

Baseline Simulation (Original BAB)

The original design exhibited ~9.4 mm peak deformation near the rear, but many areas were over-engineered. The first improvement step involved placing core at bends and removing core along the width of the car where stresses were minimal.

Iteration 1 — Skeletal Core Concept

CATIA skeletal core layout

The first iteration served as an initial proof of concept focused on exploring and understanding the capabilities of the CATIA Composites Design tool. During this phase, the foundational skeletal core structure was developed and established, providing the primary framework that will guide and support future design iterations.

Iteration 2 — Edge Reinforcement

CATIA model
Core thickness map (Rohacell 200)
Deformation: 2.66 mm (stiffer but heavier)

Edges were reinforced to improve stiffness. However, using only thick Rohacell 200 throughout resulted in higher mass (35.09 kg) than the original — confirming that a mixed-core strategy was necessary.

Iteration 3 — Mixed Core Types

Multi-density core (Rohacell + Divinycell)
Core thickness map (Rohacell 200)
Deformation: 8.88 mm (lighter, good performance)

This iteration removed even more core and introduced different core types/thicknesses. Structural performance improved while weight dropped significantly (25.88 kg) — proving that selective core removal was viable.

Iteration 4 — Final Optimized Layout

Refined core between stiffeners
Thickness optimization (Rohacell + Divinycell)
Deformation: 9.21 mm (within limit, 21.94 kg)
✅ Final iteration insight: The skeletal core from Iteration 1 already included integrated stiffeners. Recognizing redundancy, core was placed between stiffeners and additional material was removed. The final design achieves favourable deformation (≈9.2 mm) at only 21.94 kg — a 31% reduction from baseline.
